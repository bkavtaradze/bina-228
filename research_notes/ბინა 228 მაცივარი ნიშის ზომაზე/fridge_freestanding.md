# Built-in fridge-freezers for the 75.8 cm place in flat №228 (and, as a secondary section, the free-standing 70–75 cm models collected before the target changed)

Scope note. The assignment started as "free-standing fridge 66–75.8 cm wide" and was re-aimed during the work to "a built-in fridge that fills the same place well". The built-in material comes first; the free-standing findings are kept as a short last section. All prices are GEL as each product's own page showed them on 2026-10-11; saved copies of the pages are in `scratchpad/round4/pages/fridge_freestanding/` and of the makers' manuals and drawings in its `manuals/` sub-folder. No browser was used. No m² Club discount has been subtracted anywhere.

The place: 75.8 cm clear between the wall (on the left) and the kitchen cabinets (on the right), 87 cm deep to the line of the cabinet fronts, ceiling at 2.98 m. A built-in fridge stands in a furniture housing, so the figure that decides the fit is: maker's niche width + two housing sides (1.8 cm each with 18 mm board) ≤ 75.8 cm.

## Which wide built-in fridge-freezers (69–76 cm) are sold in Georgia, and does each one's niche fit 75.8 cm?

### Takeaway
Thirteen built-in fridge-freezers with a 69–70.8 cm body are on sale in Georgia, from 2,049.99 GEL (Haier HBQW5719E, 382 l) to 12,300 GEL; every maker's niche drawing that could be read asks for at least 71.0–71.4 cm of clear width, i.e. a housing at least 74.6–75.0 cm wide with 18 mm sides, so they fit the 75.8 cm place — but with only 0.8–1.2 cm to spare. The largest are the Midea MDRE554FGD01 and its twin Kuppersberg RBN 1971 (403 l) and the Haier HBW7719DT (397–400 l); no 75–76 cm built-in (Liebherr ECBN class, niche 76.2 cm) is sold here, and the Liebherr French-door units kontakt.ge does sell need 91.5 cm.

### Inferences
- "70 cm built-in" is one platform size across makers: body 69–70.8 cm, niche 71.0–71.4 cm at the minimum (up to 71.8–72.0 cm), designed for a 75 cm wide cabinet. A 75 cm cabinet in a 75.8 cm place leaves 0.8 cm; with the minimum niche of 710 mm and 18 mm sides the housing is 74.6 cm and 1.2 cm is left. The place is therefore just wide enough, with no room for error: the kitchen maker has to measure the clear width at the floor, mid-height and 2 m, because a wall that leans in by a centimetre uses the whole margin.
- The margin can be widened only on the housing, not on the fridge: 16 mm board instead of 18 mm gives 4 mm more. The „≥696“ on the Midea/Kuppersberg drawing should not be relied on as a niche width: Midea's toleranced figure on the same drawing is 714 (+6/0), in line with every other 69 cm body (710–718), and Electrolux's drawing prints a similar pair (niche „710 +8/0“ and a separate „min.695“ in a detail view). ## Can two built-in appliances stand side by side within 75.8 cm?

### Takeaway
No. Two standard 56 cm niches need 117.4 cm, a 56 cm fridge beside the one narrow built-in wine cooler found in Georgia (30 cm) needs 91.4 cm, and even a 15 cm wine cooler — which no Georgian shop sells — would need 76.4 cm. The only "pair" that fits is a 56 cm fridge housing plus a 15 cm furniture pull-out.

### Cited Findings
- Arithmetic with 18 mm board and one shared middle side: 560 + 560 + 3 × 18 = 1174 mm; 560 + 300 + 3 × 18 = 914 mm; 560 + 150 + 3 × 18 = 764 mm — each more than the 758 mm measured. The 560 mm is the standard niche width the makers print (e.g. Haier 560–568, Hisense 560–568, Teka min. 560) — [Haier installation manual](https://d15v10x8t3bz3x.cloudfront.net/Libretti/2025/11/17643382/MAN-000190138_002), [Hisense installation manual](https://partners.gorenje.com/fts/GetDigitDoc.aspx?sifra=20013391&jezik=en&tipVsebine=1&docName=rb3b280sawe_installation+manual_202607_un.pdf), [Teka installation manual](https://d7rh5s3nxmpy4.cloudfront.net/temp/RUZaSzFYcmRIWlU9/2/RBF-77360-FI-EU-InstallationManual-EN-DE-FR-IT-RO-TR.pdf)
- Pairs of full-height single-door built-ins exist in Georgia but are each 56 cm niche units: Bosch KIR81AF30U fridge 319 l (3199.99) with Bosch GIN81AEF0U freezer 211 l (3399.99) — [ee.ge fridge](https://ee.ge/macivari/bosch-kir81af30u-p9778), [ee.ge freezer](https://ee.ge/macivari/bosch-gin81aef0u-p2779); Kuppersberg SRB 1781 fridge (3792) with SFB 1781 freezer (3998), each 177.2 × 56 × 56 cm — [kuppersberg.ge fridge](https://kuppersberg.ge/chasashenebeli-matsivari-srb-1781/), [kuppersberg.ge freezer](https://kuppersberg.ge/chasashenebeli-saqhinule-sfb-1781/); Liebherr IRBAc 5190 fridge 296 l (10249.99) with SIFNe 5128 freezer 213 l (5199.99) — [kontakt.ge fridge](https://kontakt.ge/chasashenebeli-macivari-liebherr-irbac-5190), [kontakt.ge freezer](https://kontakt.ge/chasashenebeli-sayinule-liebherr-sifne-5128)

- With 16 mm board a 56 cm niche plus a 15 cm appliance niche comes to exactly 758 mm (560 + 150 + 3 × 16), i.e. zero tolerance — and the 15 cm wine cooler would have to be imported. This is not a workable plan.

### Gaps
- No Georgian shop was found selling a 15 cm built-in wine cooler; whether one can be ordered through a dealer was not established.

### Inferences
- Litres gained by going wide instead of tall-and-narrow: 382 − 284 = 98 l (Haier HBQW5719E, Bosch KBN96ADD0), 397 − 284 = 113 l (Haier HBW7719DT by its manual), 403 − 284 = 119 l (Midea, Kuppersberg). Against the 177 cm models already in the budget (243–272 l) the gain is 110–160 l.
- What stays beside a wide built-in is only a filler of at most 1.2 cm at the wall — no usable slot. What stays above is one cupboard about 85–90 cm high and the full 75.8 cm wide (and it can be deeper than the kitchen uppers, because the place is 87 cm deep).
- The upper cupboard must not close the air path: all the drawings want an outlet of at least 200 cm² above the fridge — over a 71 cm wide housing that is a slot about 3 cm high, or a channel behind the cupboard open at the ceiling — and an inlet of the same size in the plinth.
- Because the margin is 8–12 mm at best, the decision should follow a site measurement at the floor, mid-height and 2 m: if the place is under 74.6 cm anywhere (74.2 cm with 16 mm board), no wide built-in fits and a standard 56 cm niche is the fallback; between 74.6 and 75.0 cm the Haier, Bosch and Electrolux bodies (niche from 710 mm) still fit but the Midea/Kuppersberg (714 mm) does not.
- Sliding-hinge models (Midea, Haier, Hisense, Kuppersberg, Teka, Electrolux) carry the furniture door on the housing's own hinges, which suits a tall, heavy handleless front; the Bosch carries the fronts on the fridge doors and limits them to 20 kg and 19 kg — the kitchen maker must weigh the chosen fronts.

### Gaps
- The exact plinth height and front thickness of the designed kitchen were not available, so the height left above is given as a range.
- Whether the place is 75.8 cm at every height (wall plumb) has not been measured for this purpose.
- Hinge side as delivered is published only for the Haier (right) and the Electrolux twin (right); for the others it must be asked, although all state reversible doors.

### Inferences
- By the shops' own figures a free-standing 70 cm model gives about 505 l (Bosch KGN56) and a 75 cm model 521–542 l (Bosch KGN76 / KDN76), against about 445 l for the largest 60 cm models — a gain of roughly 60 l and 75–95 l. A wide built-in (382–403 l) therefore holds 120–160 l less than the largest free-standing fridge the place could take; that is the price of the furniture front.
- A 75 cm free-standing body in a 75.8 cm place would have 0.8 cm of air in total at the sides; whether that is allowed depends on each maker's manual, which was not read.

- Multi-door (French-door, cross-door) models in this band were not separated out or checked one by one.
